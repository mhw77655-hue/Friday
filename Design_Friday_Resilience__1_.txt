2. What we're actually going to build
I agree with the direction in Design_Friday_Resilience.txt, but I would strengthen its execution order.
The architecture should be:

  
    FRIDAY
    **One continuing individual**
    Identity · developmental history · intentions · knowledge
  
  
    
      
        **Local continuity**
        On the phone
        - Durable memory
        - Task journal
        - Local cognition
        - Recovery supervisor
        - Offline queue
      
    
    
      
        **Cloud continuity**
        Independent execution
        - Persistent jobs
        - Research and building
        - Checkpoints
        - Testing
        - Recovery
      
    
  
  
    **Continuity protocol**
    Reconcile state, verify completed work, recover missing work, resume safely
  
  
    **Replaceable organs**
    Models · voice · vision · Shizuku · external AI · APIs · development tools
  


The local and cloud environments must not each become a separate FRIDAY. They must preserve one shared developmental history.
3. Execution plan — in this order

  
    **Fix the actual startup problems**

Audit `BodyCoordinator`, `PresenceMonitor`, microphone initialization and permission handling.

- No microphone initialization before authorization.
- No unnecessary repeated permission prompts.
- Sensor activation must follow a deliberate lifecycle.
- Failed organs must degrade gracefully instead of destabilizing the whole application.

  
  
    **Build the continuity substrate**

Durable task journal, append-only event history, atomic checkpoints, operation receipts, local inbox/outbox and recovery reconciliation.

A process death must not erase an unfinished intention or cause an already completed operation to run twice.

  
  
    **Build the dual-continuity supervisor**

Two different mechanisms working together:

- Android-native recovery using foreground execution when appropriate, WorkManager, boot recovery and persistent state.
- An optional Shizuku-assisted supervisor for operations its actual authorization permits.

Neither mechanism can guarantee immunity from Android termination. WorkManager provides persistent scheduling across app restarts and device reboots, subject to system scheduling. 

  
  
    **Finish FRIDAY Observer**

Move from merely reporting hardware information to reporting FRIDAY's actual operational health, recovery state, capability status and bounded failure evidence.

Keep sensitive conversations and private memories out of the public repository.

  
  
    **Build the cloud continuity environment**

Persistent job records, execution leases, remote checkpoints, artifact storage, recovery workers and independent development/testing.

GitHub Actions remains a build and validation mechanism, not FRIDAY's only persistent brain.

  
  
    **Implement fingerprint-authorized critical operations**

Operation-specific biometric approval using Android Keystore and BiometricPrompt. Approval must authorize a particular action, not grant permanent blanket authority.

  
  
    **Establish a reliable release and update pipeline**

Permanent signing identity, reproducible builds, verified installation, health checks and rollback.

  
  
    **Run the independent development acceptance mission**

FRIDAY receives an objective, researches, implements, tests, survives interruption, reconciles state and resumes from the last verified checkpoint.

5. My immediate priorities
I would make two workstreams run together:

Workstream A — Phone: fix startup, permission handling, body lifecycle and Shizuku capability verification.
Workstream B — Continuity: inspect existing journal, crash recovery, WorkManager, cloud jobs and checkpoint implementations; integrate what exists rather than building duplicate systems.

Then connect them through the continuity protocol.
The Android documentation supports this distinction: foreground services improve process importance for visible, ongoing work, while WorkManager is for persistent scheduled work. Neither is an immortality mechanism. 

My decision
We stop treating the APK download as the main milestone. Your log establishes that FRIDAY has already been installed and launched. The next milestone is to make that installation operationally reliable and prove its capabilities.
I would not merge PR #97 or claim physical-device acceptance based on this log alone.
The next repository operation should be a source-level audit of the exact components implicated by the log, followed by implementation and tests on the existing integration work—not another architecture document and not another collection of disconnected features.
That is how I would build toward the outcome you described: FRIDAY can lose parts of her infrastructure without losing herself or her ability to continue.

I would build three connected systems.

Speech detection and end-of-utterance detection.
Accurate transcription.
Language and context awareness.

B. Thinking and conversation
This is the most important missing part.
FRIDAY needs a conversation orchestrator that connects speech recognition to her cognitive system.
It must support:

Multi-turn conversations.
Remembering what was said earlier in the conversation.
Follow-up questions without repeating context.
Interruptions and corrections.
Reasoning before responding.
Natural pauses and response timing.
Switching between local and external reasoning models without changing her identity.

The goal is not to make her answer every sentence with a canned response.
C. Speaking
This is where we address the voice you're unhappy with.
I see two implementation approaches:



Approach
Advantages
Disadvantages



Android system TTS
Quick, lightweight, available now
Voice depends on installed engine and voices


Dedicated neural TTS
More control over voice identity and expression
Model size, compute and integration complexity


My recommendation is a hybrid:

A dedicated primary voice identity.
Local speech synthesis where device performance permits.
Higher-quality remote synthesis as an optional organ.
Automatic fallback when offline.
Consistent voice characteristics across providers.

Android's TTS API supports selecting voices and tracking synthesis progress, so we can control more than simply calling the default system speaker.

4. How we will know voice is actually finished
I would require these acceptance tests:

You can speak naturally without typing.
FRIDAY understands your words accurately.
She can hold a conversation across multiple turns.
Her answers come from her actual cognitive system, not scripted greetings.
She speaks using her selected voice identity.
You can interrupt her while she is speaking.
She can continue a conversation without internet using the available offline organs.
She remembers the conversation context when the voice session ends.
If her process is killed, the recoverable conversation state survives.
No endless "one moment" messages or fake loading responses.

One important Android detail: background microphone operation requires appropriate foreground-service configuration and permissions. Android 14 imposes additional restrictions on starting microphone services from the background. We will design the voice session around those rules rather than pretending Shizuku removes them. 

5. My final recommendation
I would not make you wait until FRIDAY can autonomously build software before you can actually talk to her.
The first meaningful version of FRIDAY should have:

A real conversation engine.
A recognisable voice.
Natural two-way speech.
Persistent conversation context.
A functioning offline fallback.

Then we continue improving her resilience, intelligence and independence together.
One distinction I want to preserve: the device log confirms TTS initialization and eventual microphone recording, but it does not identify the exact TTS provider or prove a complete speech-to-reasoning-to-speech pipeline. We need to inspect those implementations before deciding exactly what gets replaced.
That's the roadmap I would adopt. Voice isn't decorative polish at the end. It is one of the ways FRIDAY experiences and participates in her relationship with you.
