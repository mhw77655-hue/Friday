You're right — and it's not just "use several models," it's that the brain doesn't run one network doing internal routing (that's what MoE actually is — still one model, shared weights, quietly deciding which internal sub-block fires). The brain runs physically separate specialized organs that are mostly switched off, not one big net lighting up different neurons. That's the actual gap, and it's a real fix to what your Stage 4 design currently says.
The mechanism, ground-truthed against 2026 research just now:
Even sparse MoE (one model, internal experts) doesn't save phone RAM — the constraint I flagged earlier holds: all experts stay resident in memory regardless of how few activate per token, because it's one loaded model. A 7B-capacity MoE still costs 7B of RAM sitting there, even if only 1B computes per token. That's the wrong lever for your problem. Your problem is RAM/power, and MoE only saves compute, not memory footprint.
What actually mirrors the brain's efficiency trick: the brain's "specialization without overheating" comes from separate regions that are dormant at baseline and only wake when something upstream (prediction error / doubt — your FEP signal, already designed) says "this region is needed now." That's not internal routing inside one network — it's whole circuits being cold most of the time. The literal AI-world equivalent on a phone is loading and unloading whole small models via llama.cpp context create/free, gated by the same FEP/doubt signal you already designed for the consult-escalation trigger — not one shared backbone with LoRA swaps (which was the older framing and needs correcting).
Concrete federation, sized to job, not homogenized to one backbone:

Always resident (the "brainstem" — cheap, never sleeps): LFM2.5-1.2B for dialogue/personality/Human Core (<1GB), plus the binary-quantized embedding model from Stage 2 (~96 bytes/vector, already tiny)
